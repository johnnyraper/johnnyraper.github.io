Product design

PhysicsX: Product Design for Complex Engineering Software

Product design for technically complex engineering software, helping specialist users navigate demanding workflows and make confident decisions. My work spans product discovery, interaction design, design systems, and close collaboration with engineering teams to create clearer, more consistent product experiences.

System context

PhysicsX develops AI-driven software for complex engineering workflows. Designing for this environment requires making sophisticated technical capabilities usable without oversimplifying the needs of specialist users.

My focus has been on creating clear interaction patterns and reusable product foundations that support consistency as the software evolves, while keeping decisions grounded in real engineering workflows.

My role

I operate across product discovery, interaction design, design systems, and design–engineering collaboration. This involves understanding specialist workflows, working closely with engineers and domain experts, and translating technical requirements into clear, scalable product experiences.

Alongside product work, I use modern prototyping approaches to explore ideas quickly, test interaction models, and communicate design intent more clearly with engineering teams.

Outcome

  • Improved consistency through reusable interaction patterns and shared design foundations.
  • Strengthened collaboration between design and engineering by making design intent clearer and easier to implement.
  • Contributed to scalable theming and design system foundations for evolving product needs.
  • Used rapid prototyping to explore complex workflows and improve the quality of design discussions.

Overview

The product environment spans a range of technical workflows used by engineering teams. My role has involved identifying opportunities to improve clarity, consistency, and usability across these experiences.

The work requires close collaboration across product, design, engineering, and subject-matter expertise, with decisions balancing user needs, technical constraints, and long-term product quality.

Discovery and product shaping

Many design problems in complex engineering software begin before interface design. Understanding specialist workflows, technical constraints, and user goals is essential before deciding how a product experience should work. My discovery work focuses on turning that complexity into clear product direction:

  • Lead and facilitate discussions with engineers and domain experts to understand workflows and user needs.
  • Translate technically complex requirements into clearer product experiences and interaction models.
  • Balance usability, precision, technical feasibility, and long-term scalability with multidisciplinary teams.

Design ↔ engineering alignment

A significant part of my work involves strengthening the connection between product design and implementation through reusable patterns and shared design system foundations.

  • Work directly with frontend engineers to ensure interaction and component decisions are practical to implement.
  • Create clearer shared patterns that help design and engineering work from the same intent.
  • Validate design system decisions against real product use cases rather than treating the system in isolation.

Design systems and platform foundations

  • Contributed to semantic design foundations that support theming and extensibility.
  • Established clearer visual and interaction conventions across shared interface patterns.
  • Developed reusable approaches that improve consistency and reduce repeated design decisions.

The aim is to move away from one-off interface decisions towards reusable foundations that can evolve with the product.

Structuring complex product areas

I design for specialist users working with dense information, technical controls, and multi-step workflows. The challenge is to improve clarity without removing the depth or precision those users need. My work includes:

  • Define structural patterns for navigation, information architecture, and workflow organisation.
  • Develop shared interaction models that improve consistency across complex product areas.
  • Balance precision, efficiency, and usability in technically demanding contexts.

AI-assisted prototyping

I also explore AI-assisted ways of working where they improve the design process: speeding up exploration, making prototypes more realistic, and helping teams discuss complex interactions earlier.

  • Use AI-assisted tools to explore and prototype complex interactions more quickly.
  • Create higher-fidelity prototypes that make behaviour easier to evaluate with stakeholders and engineers.
  • Experiment with ways to make design, prototyping, and implementation conversations more connected.

The goal is practical: shorten feedback loops, test ideas earlier, and improve communication between product design and engineering.

Designing for adoption

Reusable systems only create value when they fit the needs of the teams and products using them. I therefore treat adoption as part of the design problem, not as something that happens automatically after a pattern is created.

  • Ground shared patterns in real product needs and workflows.
  • Make guidance clear enough to support confident use across different contexts.
  • Balance consistency with the flexibility required by complex technical products.

Approach

  • Work across product, design, and engineering rather than treating them as separate disciplines.
  • Validate decisions against real user workflows and technical constraints.
  • Balance long-term scalability with practical delivery needs.
  • Prioritise clarity, consistency, and decision quality over short-term visual polish.

Closing

My work at PhysicsX combines product design, systems thinking, and close collaboration with engineering. The common thread is making complex technical software clearer, more consistent, and easier to evolve without losing the depth required by expert users.
