Product design

PhysicsX: Product Design for AI-Driven Engineering Systems

Product design across AI-driven engineering software, helping engineers navigate complex workflows and make confident technical decisions. My work spans product discovery, design systems, platform foundations, and AI-assisted workflows, improving how product, design, and engineering teams collaborate to build and scale sophisticated software.

System context

PhysicsX develops AI-driven engineering software used to support simulation, optimisation, and technical decision-making across complex engineering workflows. As the platform expanded across multiple products and teams, the challenge became larger than individual screens or features.

Product workflows were becoming increasingly sophisticated, requiring greater consistency, clearer interaction patterns, and stronger alignment between design, engineering, and product strategy. Success depended not only on designing interfaces, but on creating systems that could scale with the organisation and support future product growth.

My role

I operate across product discovery, platform design, design systems, and design–engineering alignment. My work involves understanding complex engineering workflows, collaborating with engineers and domain experts, shaping product direction, and creating scalable foundations that improve how design decisions move from concept through implementation.

Alongside product work, I have explored AI-assisted workflows and prototyping approaches that help reduce ambiguity between design intent and production software.

Outcome

  • Improved consistency across multiple engineering products through shared interaction patterns and scalable system foundations.
  • Reduced ambiguity between design and engineering through clearer implementation contracts and closer collaboration.
  • Introduced semantic theming foundations to support future product evolution.
  • Explored AI-assisted design-to-code workflows to accelerate iteration and improve implementation fidelity.

Overview

The platform spans multiple applications and workflows for engineering teams. As product areas evolved, opportunities emerged to improve consistency, reduce friction, and create clearer foundations for future growth.

Much of the challenge was organisational as well as visual: aligning product, design, and engineering around shared patterns while ensuring decisions remained grounded in real implementation constraints and customer needs.

Discovery and product shaping

Many of the problems addressed within PhysicsX begin long before interface design. Engineering workflows often involve specialised domain knowledge, technical constraints, and competing stakeholder needs. A key part of my role is working with engineers, product leaders, and subject matter experts to understand how work is performed today and identify opportunities for improvement. Here are some examples of how I approach this discovery work and use it to shape product direction:

  • Lead and facilitate discussions with engineers and domain experts to understand workflow challenges.
  • Translate technical complex requirements into clearer product experiences and interaction models.
  • Identify trade-offs between usability, precision, implementation effort, and long-term scalability, working closely with product managers and engineers across multidisciplinary teams.

Design ↔ engineering alignment

A significant part of my work has involved evolving PhysicsX's design system and platform foundations to support multiple products, teams, and implementation contexts.

  • Work directly with frontend engineers to define how design decisions, component behaviours, and system foundations are implemented.
  • Document clearer relationships between design assets, frontend libraries, and consuming applications.
  • Validate decisions against real product workflows and implementation constraints rather than treating the design system as an isolated library.

Design systems and platform foundations

  • Introduced semantic token structures to better support theming and future extensibility.
  • Defined clearer roles for surfaces, text, borders, and interaction states.
  • Developed reusable patterns intended to reduce duplication and improve implementation consistency.

This work shifts decision-making away from one-off visual solutions towards a more durable framework capable of supporting multiple teams and evolving product needs.

Structuring complex product areas

I design software for engineers operating in highly technical environments. Many workflows involve dense information, technical controls, and multi-step decision-making processes. The challenge is to improve clarity and usability without sacrificing the depth required by specialist users. This is where I:

  • Define product-wide structural patterns for navigation, information architecture, and workflow organisation.
  • Explore shared interaction models and archetypes that support visual and functional consistency across products.
  • Balance precision, efficiency, and usability within technically demanding contexts.

AI-assisted prototyping

A growing area of focus has been integrating AI into the design process to accelerate exploration, reduce implementation ambiguity, and create more realistic prototypes. Rather than using AI as a content-generation tool, I explore how it can support complex product thinking, interface design, and closer collaboration with engineering, around the following areas:

  • Use AI-assisted workflows to rapidly prototype complex interactions and workflows.
  • Generate implementation-aware prototypes that more closely reflect production behaviour.
  • Explore how design systems, frontend code, and documentation can become more directly connected.

This work sits at the intersection of product design and engineering, exploring ways to shorten feedback loops and improve the transition from idea to implementation.

Adoption and organisational challenges

One of the more important discoveries has been that system quality alone does not guarantee adoption. Customer needs, implementation pressures, and varying levels of frontend maturity all influence whether teams adopt shared patterns or revert to one-off solutions. It's especially important to understand the real-world contexts in which teams are operating within:

  • Identify friction points between design intent and implementation reality.
  • Explore earlier intervention points to improve adoption and consistency.
  • Focus on improving how teams engage with the system, not only the system itself.

Approach

  • Operate across product, design, and engineering, bringing shared alignment, rather than treating them as separate disciplines.
  • Validate decisions against real user workflows and implementation constraints.
  • Balance long-term scalability with practical delivery needs.
  • Focus on clarity, consistency, and decision-making quality over short-term visual optimisation.

Closing

This work focused on helping a rapidly evolving AI platform scale effectively. The impact extended beyond individual interfaces, improving how product, design, and engineering collaborate, creating clearer foundations for future growth, and enabling teams to deliver complex software with greater consistency and confidence.
